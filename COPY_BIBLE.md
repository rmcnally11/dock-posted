# Copy bible — talk like a person on the dock

Last rewrite: 1 September 2026.
Companion to the pamphlet *Talk like a person on the dock*.

Warm is not cute. Honest is not cold.

## The two home lines

**Dock Posted**
What they wrote on the pump. If they didn’t, ask the dock.

**On This Water**
This morning on your water.

Those two sentences do the work the old slogans were trying to do with a clenched jaw. Put them on the landing. Put them on the envelope. Then get out of the way.

## The warm-puppy test

Holding a warm puppy is a test, not a tone.

It means close, alive, and safe in your hands. It does not mean bows, emojis, or “we’re so excited.” A puppy that will not look you in the eye is still a bad dog. Warm copy still tells the truth.

Read every line out loud to someone who keeps a skiff at Seabrook. If they tilt their head, it is firmware. If they already say it at the bait stand, keep it.

## Six rules

1. **Write at 4:40 a.m.** If you wouldn’t text it to a buddy who asked “we going?”, don’t put it on the site.
2. **Name the thing.** A price is a price. A dock is a dock. A fish is a fish. Stop promoting the container.
3. **Second person, contractions.** You, your, we’ll, hasn’t. No “users.” No “the product working, not failing.”
4. **One breath.** A twelve-year-old on the bow should get the sentence before the second wake.
5. **Keep the salt people already use.** The glass. First light. On the hose. Marks. Those are warm because they’re old.
6. **Never invent.** No made-up pump number. No promised bite. Warm does not mean comforting lies.

## Do

- Use the place name. Galveston, not the Texas desk.
- Use the time of day. This morning. First light. Truck day.
- Use you / your / we’ll.
- Put the phone on the card when there is no price.
- One idea per sentence.
- Keep the glass, marks, on the hose, first light.
- Say the dollar if there is a dollar. $299 a season. $29 a year.
- Let a blank be a blank.
- Keep the companies apart in public. Dock Posted does not take a fuel order.

## Don’t

- Call, Pin, Board, Run, Desk, Theater, Hunt, Clock, YOLO, Field Brief.
- “The product working, not failing.”
- “Users.” “Instrument.” “Cadence.” “Elect a coast.”
- Invented Regular or diesel.
- A promised bite.
- Roll-up talk on a public page.
- Jokes that need a glossary.
- Cute. No paws, no “family of brands,” no mission statement.
- “We’re so excited to announce.”
- Wholesale, Steward, or Waterdog gallons on the Dock Posted home page.

## Page-level voice

### Dock Posted home
Short. Dark water. One promise. Two buttons: **See today’s docks** and **I was there**. No weekly scoreboard speech.

### Your dock (`/pin`)
Talk to the marina manager like a neighbor, not a SaaS. “Boats look here before they leave. You write the number when the truck comes. $299 a season.”

### This trip (`/run`)
Talk to the trailer-dad on Friday night. “Before you leave, see what a tank will cost. If they haven’t put a number up, we leave it blank.”

### Yard seats (`/haul-out`)
Calm. No weather theater. “When they name a storm, yards fill up. Here’s who still has a seat. You call them. We don’t pull her.”

### I was there (`/report`)
One screen. “You were there. What did they have on the hose.”

### What’s in the hose (`/safe-fuel`)
Plain. “If the sticker says 15% ethanol, walk away.”

### On This Water home
Place names. Morning words. “This morning on Galveston.” Never “pick a desk — the brief is one water.”

### The line (`/morning`)
One sentence a person can paste into a group text. Nothing else on that page is required.

### Saturday
Only the coasts they asked for. Open with the morning, not a magazine lede.

### Your book
A notebook, not a product. “Write the fish. The wind and the tide stay with it.”

## Year gate (do not break)

Year 1 public mouth is the board and the morning. Waterdog gallons wait on GATE 5. Steward stays quiet. Wholesale is a locked door. Warm language is not permission to sell next year’s business with this year’s sentence.
